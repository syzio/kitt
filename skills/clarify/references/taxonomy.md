# Coverage categories

Scan the target against the matching list and mark each category Clear, Partial or Missing. Only a Partial or Missing category whose answer changes the outcome becomes a question.

## Spec mode

1. **Functional scope and behavior.** Core user goals and success criteria, explicit out-of-scope items, and distinct user roles.
2. **Domain and data model.** Entities, attributes and relationships, identity and uniqueness rules, lifecycle and state transitions, and data volume and scale.
3. **Interaction and UX flow.** Critical user journeys, error, empty and loading states, accessibility, and localization.
4. **Non-functional qualities.** Performance targets, scalability limits, reliability and recovery, observability (logs, metrics, traces), security and privacy (authentication, authorization, data protection, threat assumptions), and compliance.
5. **Integrations and dependencies.** External services and how they fail, import and export formats, and protocol and versioning assumptions.
6. **Edge cases and failure handling.** Negative scenarios, rate limits, and conflicts such as concurrent edits or duplicate submissions.
7. **Constraints and trade-offs.** Technical constraints (language, storage, hosting) and rejected alternatives.
8. **Terminology.** Canonical terms, and the synonyms and old terms to avoid.
9. **Completion signals.** Testable acceptance criteria and a measurable definition of done.
10. **Placeholders.** TODOs, unresolved decisions, and vague adjectives with no number, such as "fast", "secure", "intuitive" or "robust".

## Document mode

These categories fit any document built together, such as a licence, policy, contract, runbook, config or proposal.

1. **Purpose and audience.** Who the document is for, and what it must achieve.
2. **Scope and boundaries.** What it covers, and what it explicitly doesn't.
3. **Parties and roles.** Who is bound, who decides, and who is affected.
4. **Permissions and obligations.** What is allowed, required and prohibited.
5. **Exceptions and edge cases.** Special circumstances, and how the document handles each one.
6. **Consequences.** What happens on breach, failure, expiry or termination.
7. **Terminology.** Defined terms, used the same way throughout.
8. **Jurisdiction, dates and versions.** Include these where they apply.
9. **Completion.** What "final" looks like, and who signs off.

## Grill mode

Grill mode has no fixed list. Map the decision tree of the plan, design or idea instead. Use these prompts to find branches you might miss.

1. **Goal.** What problem it solves, for whom, and how you will know it worked.
2. **Scope.** What is in, what is out, and what the smallest useful version is.
3. **Approach.** The main choice, and the alternatives it rules out.
4. **Users and stakeholders.** Who uses it, who decides, and who is affected.
5. **Risks and failure.** What could go wrong, and what happens then.
6. **Constraints.** Time, budget, skills, tools and rules that limit the choices.
7. **Sequence.** What comes first, and what waits on what.

## Constitution additions

The constitution may add coverage categories for its domain. Append them to the matching list and scan them the same way.
